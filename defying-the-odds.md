On a mission to defy the odds
The story of why I'm choosing to start over by going back to sofware engineering despite the worst market ever for juniors. 
But first, let's talk about the elephant in the room: the ghost of the junior/entry level path. 
Where are all the juniors roles
Well, after the covid wave and the senior only hiring wave, came the AI wave. Each wave reduced opportunities for juniors that were already slim in the market. Keep in mind, this is for recent graduates, so what about career transitioners? Well, even fewer opportunities left. If internal transitions at their current workplace isn't possible, the chances drop exponentially. 
In 2018 when I was initially looking for a role, it was already hard. The opportunities in my city weren't that many and much less on a decent salary. So eventually, in need of providing for the family, due to financial obligations, I found the next "best" thing through a role in technical support engineering. It wasn't meant to be forever - I was undecided after joining IMS because I had such a great experience. Something was still calling me to go back. 
Fast forward to 2023, a decision was made - it's time to go back. But, where are the roles? Well, they are even less scarce now and fun fact, I'm now 5 years post graduation. As someone who also graduated late, this only meant one thing : you're getting older, get a move on lady! 
Unfortunately, it's now 2026 and the market hasn't gotten that much better. Companies are still concentrated on finding primarily senior engineers and higher. Senior engineers are themselves having a tough time finding a role now, and layoffs have become like a fashion trend - sadly one that doesn't quite seem like it wants to go out of style. 
With all, one would wonder: why would I want to still to make the transition? If the deck is stacked up against me (consider age, lack of internal mobility and the market), why even bother? Why not stick to the road I'm on? Simple: it's not my road. 
Just because I took a detour off a highway that landed me 100 miles in the wrong direction, doesn't mean the road ahead isn't mine. For me it's about the challenge of the role - the challenge of solving problems I care about, being part of something bigger, getting challenged routinely on the job while doing something that truly excites me. Sure, sometimes it makes me want to pull out my hair but in the best of ways.
