# What I’ve Learned After 18 Months of Getting Back To Coding While Working Full-Time (Part 1)

This week, your friendly neighborhood software engineer is back with another post. But before we dive into the whirlwind of balancing a 9–5 with side projects and learnings, let me take you on a quick journey through my past.

## 🎓 The Early Days: Breaking Barriers

In 2012, I embarked on my software development journey armed with a computer science technical diploma, followed by a Bachelor’s degree. Out of a cohort of 40+ students, I was one of only four women: two of us graduated, one dropped out after the first semester, and one graduated a term later. It was a challenging time, being one of the few women in the field with limited community and limited workplace support. But I was determined not to let that define me.

## 💼 The Detour: Getting To Know Customers At A Deeper Level

After some time, I transitioned into customer-facing roles that allowed me to blend my technical skills, especially my love for SQL, with direct interaction with clients. This shift felt somewhat natural, as I’d been working with customers since the age of 14, legally I might add(yes, you read that right!). Whether it was running my personal training business or working in health tech, I thrived in roles that kept me close to the people I was helping.

## 🔄 And Years Later

But here’s the twist: after years of customer interactions, around 2019, while working at Intelerad Medical Systems with a fantastic group of developers, I felt the pull to return to my roots: solving problems through code and building things that could make a real impact. The idea of creating fun, functional solutions for the very people I’d been serving was too enticing to ignore. Shifting to a more people leader role in 2023 was the final confirmation to call me back to “home sweet home” again. While I love mentoring, sharing nuggets of wisdom about career growth, and sharing my knowledge with others, management was not for me. Sometimes, a U-turn in life may take a while before happening. That year was my personal U-turn.

## 😰 But First, I Must Brush Up My Skills

Before getting back to my first love though, I needed to spend some time on “refreshing” some of my skills… You know, the ones I used less in my day-to-day, and I also needed to learn a new programming language, specifically Ruby On Rails. Why? Well, initially it was a great choice because my workplace uses Rails, so it kind of made sense. This was exciting! I was embarking on a brand new yet familiar journey at the same time, it may not have been the “best” time to do so, at least according to the job and tech market. However, being a persistent and determined person, that was not about to stop me! It’s been an exciting and fun journey at times but if I’m being honest, it also came with its set of particular challenges, which I’ll be diving into here.

## 🏗️ So About Those Challenges

There’s no doubt that trying to build up skills, work on side projects, having a side hustle, and going through job hunting can be very challenging on their own, but building in one career while “operating” in another is a unique beast. Over the last eighteen months, the challenges I’ve experienced on this journey revolved around:

- Time management: carving out hours after work, on weekends, and on holidays
- Learning a new language
- Mental fatigue: switching from team lead responsibilities to deep technical learning after work
- Staying motivated when progress feels slow and results aren’t immediate
- Dealing with distractions, burnout, grief, loss, impostor syndrome, and self-doubt.

## 🕰️ Time: It’s Expensive

We all have limited time and spend on average a third of our day in the workplace, so making progress outside of work requires intentionality, organization, and commitment. For me, in the first twelve months, I spent at least 4 evenings over weekdays buried in learning, projects, and reading.
On the weekends, Sundays became my sacred day for uninterrupted project work — headphones on, world off, phone on Do Not Disturb. Rinse and repeat!

Need to listen to a podcast as part of learning? I did that too during my morning or afternoon walks, and most times while commuting (even on my way to my Salsa dance class!). Every single time I took the train, you’d find me with my laptop, iPad, or something in my ear. And no, it wasn’t music.

As you can imagine, my calendar became my best friend with blocks for learning, including the ones during my commutes unless the commute was the dedicated time to make phone calls — I know, I know, I became that person walking in the streets with entire conversations in public but I promise I was never loud.

## 📚 Learning a new language: Ruby on Rails

Now this part was very exciting and challenging! I’m not Ruby On Rails and in fact I’m still learning more bit by bit, but coming from Java as my first programming language, this was… hmm.. can we just say “special”? After all, with Java, I was used to static typing and verbose syntax. Transitioning to Ruby and Rails where everything is an object, was interesting.

Learning Rails was like stepping into somewhat unknown territory for me. Outside of my knowledge of Model-view-controller architecture, everything was absolutely brand new to me, and many things still are. I’m embracing the challenge and the complexity of this framework. While some things can feel overwhelming at times along the way, this was exactly the kind of challenge I was craving!

To navigate this journey, I joined an online community and the Montreal Ruby community where I’ve been able to lean on other folks, receive feedback, and learn alongside them.

## 🧠 Mental Fatigue

As you can imagine, all that learning, planning, organizing, and task switching throughout the day can get quite taxing mentally.

Imagine this: from 8 AM to 4 PM, I’m a fearless team lead who’s extinguishing fires, coaching teammates, answering a barrage of questions, getting into technical investigations, and pushing on team goals. Then, as the clock strikes evening, I put on my coder cape and dive into the deep end of technical learning.

Ok, it’s time for a confession: I much prefer to immerse myself in deep technical learning after my second cup of coffee, so around 11 AM, but you can see, however, with work commitments, that timing doesn’t quite align. So, come evening, after surviving the heat of the workday, I turn up some smooth jazz or salsa music (depending on my energy levels) and battle the fatigue to get something…anything done. And by “anything,” I mean you might find me clocking out close to midnight. At least, that was how I was managing it.

Reality check: sometimes, a nap was necessary. Other times, a nap wouldn’t cut it, and full rest was required.

## ➡️ So what’s next

Sixteen months into this journey, I’ve faced my fair share of late nights, mental fatigue, and the constant tug-of-war between work and learning. It hasn’t been easy but it’s been totally worth it. The excitement of building, learning, and growing again has reminded me why I fell in love with technology in the first place.

But of course, pushing this hard comes at a cost!

In **Part 2**, next week, I’ll share the strategies I’ve used to stay grounded and protect my energy while still making progress. From intentional rest to building sustainable rhythms, I’ll talk about the practices that have helped me keep going when things got tough.

Stay tuned.💡
